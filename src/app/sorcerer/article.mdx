# Logistics simulation

<StormBoids />

Weather is a consistent exogenous headache for Walmart or UPS; they have in-house meteorologists and data scientists cooking up forecasts. It does seem obvious that there’s a market for on-site sensors at warehouses, and Sorcerer should happily sell those, but I think the real trick is in reversing the flow of data.

Instead of sending Walmart data and forecasts they have to interpret, they could send *us* their distribution center or drone logistics, and we simulate how distribution is going to be impacted by weather— and send back a logistics solution with route corrections, timing shifts or a go/no go.

Sorcerer can just solve the problem directly by sending FDEs armed with the best data and models, rather than tangentially solving Wally’s problem by giving them a raw feed. Would we need to learn a heck of a lot about massive corporate supply chains and drone aviation? Yes. Would it be hard? Yes. Could we do it? Yes!

If Sorcerer became adept at this, there is a long tail of *really* interesting consumers who need bespoke weather and logistics solutions. Think of Zipline catapulting critical human blood deliveries, or rain.aero’s autonomous helicopters, or offshore turbine construction, or rail carriers moving ethanol.

# In-situ buoys

<PixelBuoy />

Ocean data is wildly undercollected. We rely on satellites (RIP NOAA-18, we hardly knew ye) and a very sparse network of sensors. There are players in this space, but they don’t have atmospherics *and* floats. Sensor fusion by 2030 would mean that Sorcerer could come to dominate hurricane monitoring or shipping route planning.

Imagine an in-situ monitor in the Bay; there are serious engineering challenges to overcome, but the potential good is massive. There are environmental use cases (tagging whales or monitoring reef health) and defense applications (is that a submarine?), not to mention augmenting natural disaster forecasting.

If Sorcerer were cracked at nowcasting currents and ocean winds, we could sell the data to Panthalassa or offshore wind farms (or trade on it!) and shipping firms. We could track IUU and its impact; we could track albedo and measure geoengineering trials.

I *love* the idea of Sorcerer as the definitive nervous system for the world. There is such a dearth of sensors relative to the challenges the world need to meet; let’s fix that.

# Community launches

I have a few friends who are weather nerds. They would probably work at NOAA for free; in fact, they’d probably *pay* NOAA to work there. They are already launching micro balloons themselves.

Let’s put ‘em to work!

Sorcerer should have a community launch program for a few very good reasons:

1. Recruiting; hire an illustrator to hand out mission patches or stickers at a launch, and people will be falling over themselves to work here.
2. Goodwill with NWS & NOAA never hurts
3. Community evangelism and serendipity is a powerful thing; PostHog is very effective at getting their fans to talk their employers into buying the product. You never know who has latent demand.
4. Photo ops :D
5. Launch coverage in hard to access areas

Relatedly, Sorcerer could sponsor high school/college meteorology clubs with hardware and coaching. What if there was a collegiate engineering competition for balloon design?!
